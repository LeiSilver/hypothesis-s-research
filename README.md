# Li–Liu spectral Hypothesis S

This repository contains the English research manuscript and the code and data for its finite-modulus certificates. Hypothesis S remains open.

## Current proof status under Dirichlet GRH

The manuscript uses global Dirichlet GRH, including RH for the zeta function. Its notation $\mathrm{GRH}(N)$ records when the proof only needs the zero-location result for the modulus in question. The target in (1.1) is $\|T_N\|/\mathfrak S(N)\to0$ through **all** even $N$, taking the supremum over unit tests whose supports may vary with $N$. Equation (1.1) uses the formulation allowing an arbitrarily slowly growing auxiliary function. [GRH verification record](https://math.mit.edu/~drew/grh/)

Corollary 12.9 refines the mixed-matrix condition at each sufficiently large even modulus. If $P_N(A)\to0$, the far matrix can discard every proper prime power in its small coordinate, along with all ordinary primes up to a cutoff $X_N\to\infty$ specified by the prime-only upper spectral bound. This reduction requires no rate for $P_N(A)\to0$. Decay of the near matrix and of the retained far prime rows still needs proof on general moduli.

Theorem 12.10 gives a zero-count version of the remaining far test. With $b=\log(2A)$ and $T=Lb(\log\log L)^2/(\mathfrak S(N)(\log L)^2)$, it uses only ordinates $1/T<|\gamma|\le T$ of the inducing primitive Dirichlet characters. Here $T=o(\log N)$ and $(\log N)/T\to\infty$. The discarded ranges and rounding within the specified bins cost $O(1/\log\log L)$ at each modulus under GRH. Only differences of bin counts from their averages over even twists enter the pairing. A uniform estimate for the retained weighted sum is still needed.

Theorem 12.11 bounds the contribution from bin counts shared by characters of the same primitive conductor. At every even modulus, subtracting the mean at each conductor changes either mixed prime form by $O_\epsilon(N^{-1/2+\epsilon})$ under GRH. The theorem also permits averages within smaller groups of twists at fixed conductor. The remaining far estimate concerns variation among characters inside these groups.

Corollary 12.12 also applies to $P_N(A)$ itself. Decompose its character square into the average covariance within each primitive conductor and the product of the conductor means. The latter costs $O((\log N)^{-2})$ in unnormalized form norm whenever either input is supported below $X_*=(\varphi(N)/(\tau(N)^2(\log N)^6))^2=N^{2-o(1)}$. The within-conductor covariance on general supports and the means with two large inputs remain in the form.

Theorem 12.13 approximates the conductor-mean term on arbitrary supports using only differences of the two progressions $+1$ and $-1$ at divisors $e\mid N$ with $N/e\le R$. For any fixed $D>0$, the positive semidefinite residual has unnormalized norm $O_D(L^{-D})$, where $\log R=(D+4)(\log L)^2/\log\log L$. In the support threshold of Corollary 12.12 the new divisor factor is $\sum_{t\mid N,\,t\le R}2^{\omega(t)}$, where $\omega(t)$ counts distinct prime factors. With $D=2$, the new threshold is at least $L^2 X_*$ at every sufficiently large even modulus. Comparing the within-conductor covariance and the retained high-input term with the diagonal and continuous model remains open.

Equation (12.23) evaluates that retained term without a matrix inverse. It is a positively weighted sum of squared differences between the weighted counts with $\gcd(n-1,N)=N/t$ and $\gcd(n+1,N)=N/t$, over $t\mid N$ with $t\le R$ and nonzero weight. In $P_N(A)$ these counts use ordinary primes. Extending the sum to every divisor recovers the exact conductor-mean form up to $O_\epsilon(N^{-1+\epsilon})$ under GRH.

Theorem 12.14 gives an equivalent test for the entire prime-only norm, including inputs between $A$ and the modulus. Fix $0<\rho<1$. Keep the odd characters whose local conductor exponent is maximal at each odd $p\mid N$ above $(\log N)^\rho$, and apply the same local weights to the continuous shifts. Through all even moduli the resulting normalized norm tends to zero if and only if $P_N(A)$ does. On any family defined by $\mathfrak S(N)\le C$ one may impose this condition at every odd prime divisor. The mixed matrix still enters the full criterion.

Corollary 12.15 bounds the effect of subtracting the mean at each exact conductor on individual arithmetic shift lines. For $J$ prescribed shifts of arbitrary sizes the change costs $O(J(\log N)^2\tau(N)^2/\varphi(N))$ in unnormalized norm, uniformly in the supports. The diagonal alone costs $O_\epsilon(N^{-1+\epsilon})$. The discrepancy between the centered arithmetic term and the continuous model on these lines still requires an estimate.

The additive version of Theorem 12.14 uses the usual weighted prime sine sums. Only frequencies whose reduced denominator contains the full power of each selected prime are needed, with the corresponding $1/p$ weights in the continuous shifts and the diagonal removed.

Theorem 12.16 gives a single full-space test that includes the mixed inputs. It uses the same sine sums over all odd prime powers at every denominator. Keeping only fractions of reduced denominator $N/d$ with $d$ in the specified range changes the unnormalized remainder by $O_D((\log N)^{-D})$ at each even modulus. Corollary 12.17 also gives a two-way locally primitive additive criterion for the full norm. Unnormalized decay of the remainder restricted to exact denominator $N$ is sufficient on all even moduli; the converse is proved on families with bounded singular series.

Theorem 12.18 evaluates the sampled major arcs for both physical inputs at most $N^{1.01}$. On the retained inputs they reproduce the continuous model and the explicit diagonal coefficient $H_Q=\sum_{r\le Q}\mu(r)^2/\varphi(r)$, with $Q=N^{1/12}$. The remaining estimate on this window is a minor-arc sine square minus the diagonal of weight $v-H_Q$, where $v$ is the log coordinate. The full-space estimates still require cancellation on the other support ranges as well.

For tests on $[N^{0.98},N^{1.01}]$, the grid points on arcs centered at reduced fractions with denominators between $N^{1/16}$ (excluded) and $N^{1/12}$ (included) contribute $(\log N)/48$ times the log-coordinate inner product, with unnormalized error $O(N^{-1/100})$. This band estimate is pointwise in the even modulus.

Theorem 12.19 averages the GRH progression error over all reduced numerators at a given denominator. On $x,y\le RN$ with $R$ fixed, this evaluates arcs up to denominator $\sqrt N/(\log N)^C$ with any prescribed logarithmic accuracy. For a sufficiently short first input it bounds the same arcs with arbitrary second input, as quantified in (12.34). On a fixed macroscopic interval $[sN,RN]$, Corollary 12.20 shows that these arcs carry asymptotically half the sampled sine energy for unit tests with log-coordinate $L^2$ norm bounded below. For the complementary grid the error bound is $O(\mathfrak S(N))$. Hypothesis S requires the difference from the explicit remaining diagonal to be $o(\mathfrak S(N))$.

Theorem 12.21 identifies the minor-arc error with a quadrature error: the pairing on the frequency grid minus its integral over the circle. Subtracting the Goldston–Yıldırım Ramanujan approximation from each Mangoldt weight gives the same error on integer inputs, up to the stated precision. Theorem 12.22 uses unequal subtraction levels on separated inputs. In particular, at $V=N^{1/2}$ and $W=N^{3/2}$ it permits the original Mangoldt weight on the smaller input and subtracts the approximation through $\lfloor N^{1/4-\kappa}\rfloor$ on the larger one, with $\kappa$ as in the theorem. The remaining correlation still needs a bound.

Theorem 12.23 also gives a full-operator reduction. On log packets whose indices satisfy $0\le j<k$ and $k-j\ge(2D+12)\log\log N$, the larger prime weight can retain only Möbius divisors above $\lfloor e^{(k-j)/2}(\log N)^{-D-4}\rfloor$. The sum of the errors over all such packet pairs, including their continuous terms and transposes, has unnormalized norm $O_D((\log N)^{-D})$. The larger input has no support ceiling. Cancellation among the retained long divisors remains open.

Theorem 12.24 proves cancellation in part of the long-divisor sum left by Theorem 12.23, at every sufficiently large even modulus. Write $V,W$ for the smaller and larger input scales. For $V\le N^{1/8}$ and $N^{2-\nu}\le VW\le N^{2+\nu}$ with some fixed $\nu>0$, the cutoff increases to $\lfloor N^{1/20}\sqrt{W/V}\rfloor$. Every subinterval of the newly discarded divisors, with the original Möbius-logarithm weights, has unnormalized error $O(N^{-c})$ for some fixed $c>0$. For $V\le N^{1/64}$ the exponent $1/20$ can be increased to $1/16$. Formula (12.39) gives the tradeoff up to smaller-input exponent $3/8$ (excluded). At $VW\asymp N^2$ the complementary factors left in the first case are $O(N^{19/20})$. Their contribution and the other support ranges still enter the full hypothesis.

Theorem 12.25 uses Dirichlet GRH in the smaller prime coordinate. On the separated packets of Theorem 12.23 it also permits a cutoff of $\lfloor N/(\sqrt W(\log N)^{D+8})\rfloor$ whenever this is larger, with full-operator error $O_D((\log N)^{-D})$. For example, at $V\asymp N^{4/5}$, $W\asymp N^{6/5}$, the power in the cutoff rises from $1/5$ to $2/5$, up to the stated logarithmic factors. On the critical product these two theorems give no extra power over the cutoff in Theorem 12.23 for $N^{3/8}\le V\le N^{2/3}$.

## Research checkpoint · 28 November 2026

Theorem 12.8 removes the continuous main term and all short divisors from the fixed-window integrals at each sufficiently large even modulus, without GRH. On every sum or difference line of shift $bN$ it leaves pairs $d\mid m$, $e\mid n$ with their Möbius signs, where both divisors exceed $\sqrt N\exp(-\sqrt{\log N})$, $\max(d,e)>N^{20/39-\epsilon}$ and $de>N^{45/44-\epsilon}$ for fixed $0<\epsilon<1/100$. The normalized error saves any fixed power of $\log N$ uniformly over interval endpoints, and remains that small in the weighted integral at zero. Cancellation of the retained sums is open. In the original prime-pair integrals, omitting proper powers and scaled inputs $u\le y$ costs $O_R(y^{3/2}+N^{-1/2}(\log N)^3)$ in norm.

Theorem 12.7 characterizes pointwise norm decay on every fixed window $x,y\le RN$ by $\lceil2R\rceil-1$ explicit one-dimensional integrals. Each uses one fixed triangular test supported in a half-modulus cell. Its signed prime-pair counts and continuous model give a cumulative function $P_j(t)$, and the integral of $|P_j(t)|^2/(a(t)t^3)$ is exactly the squared dual norm for that test. The sum $V_N(R)^2$ tends to zero if and only if the full physical window norm does, without GRH. At $R=1$ this requires a single integral. Proving these integrals tend to zero at every even modulus remains open.

Corollary 13.40 gives unconditional mean-square decay for $\min(x,y)\le N^\eta$ for every fixed $0<\eta<2/21$, uniformly in the critical caps $N^2\le S\le N^2(\log N)^B$, on a positive-density subfamily of the factored moduli. It consists of the even squarefree moduli in $[Y,2Y]$ with prime factors at most $Y^{\kappa(\eta)}$. Stadlmann's level of distribution $21/40$ supplies the prime progression estimate uniformly in each signed smaller input. Within the proof that avoids GRH, Theorem 13.39 leaves $N^\eta<\min(x,y)<N^{4/17+\epsilon}$ on that subfamily.

Theorem 13.39 proves unconditional mean-square norm decay over **all even moduli** in $[Y,2Y]$ for the sharp restriction $xy\le S$, $\min(x,y)\ge N^{4/17+\epsilon}$. The saving is any fixed power of $\log Y$, uniformly in $0<S\le N^2(\log N)^B$. On dyadic scales $X\le V$, the proof uses the smaller prime sum away from small denominators and the larger prime sum on the remaining annuli. The latter estimate follows from Harm’s short-interval mean square in arithmetic progressions, using zero-density estimates of Chen, Gupta and Li and of Jutila. This works at $X\ge V^{2/15+\delta}$. For the argument independent of GRH, Theorem 13.38 and this estimate leave the smaller inputs between $N^\eta$ and $N^{4/17+\epsilon}$ for each fixed $0<\eta<2/1501$ for unconditional critical-product decay on the enlarged factored family.

Theorem 13.38 proves unconditional mean-square norm decay for pairs with $\min(x,y)\le N^\eta$ for any fixed $0<\eta<2/1501$ and $xy\le S$ on an enlarged family of factored even moduli $N\in[Y,2Y]$. This holds uniformly for $N^2\le S\le N^2(\log N)^B$, with mean square $O((\log Y)^{-D})$ for every fixed $B,D$. The family allows composite factors of sizes $Y^{0.039}$ to $Y^{0.041}$ and $Y^{0.169}$ to $Y^{0.171}$ and contains a positive-density subfamily outside the earlier two-prime family. Taking the mean square separately in each residue before summing the smaller weights makes the loss depend on the product $xy$. Under global Dirichlet GRH the final assertion of Theorem 13.38 bounds the mean square of the supremum over these caps on the enlarged family for arbitrary inputs. Theorem 13.3 gives the earlier fixed-cap bound on the original family.

Theorem 12.6 gives the pointwise estimate $w_R\ll R^2\sqrt{a_R}+R^3a_R$ for every large even modulus and every $R\ge1$. Here $a_R$ is the upper spectral bound and $w_R$ is the two-sided norm on $x,y\le RN$. Thus $R^4a_R\to0$ suffices for window norm decay, and for fixed $R$ the full-form discrepancy on each sum and difference line is $O_R(\sqrt{a_R})$. The proof also bounds $a_R$ from below by the square root of the sum of cubed gap lengths between sampled pairs on $u+v=1$, with $1/4\le u\le1/3$. In particular $a_R\gg(\log N)^2/(N\mathfrak S(N))$, so the growing-window criterion can apply only if $R=o((N\mathfrak S(N)/(\log N)^2)^{1/4})$. These estimates hold for the physical forms without GRH. An upper bound tending to zero at every large even modulus remains open.

Theorem 12.5 separates the two signs of the prime-only form defining $P_N(A)$ and of the full normalized form under modulus-wise GRH. For either form, decay is equivalent to a uniform upper bound tending to zero and two lower bounds on explicit spline spaces. The lower tests have total dimension $O(L^{3/2}\log L/\sqrt{\mathfrak S(N)})$, with $L=\log N$, and need no inputs below $N/4$. The smallest generalized eigenvalue on each space measures its negative deviation. The proof uses the positivity of the character square to bound negative deviations on the discarded directions. The global upper bound and the lower bounds on the two retained spaces remain open.

Theorem 12.4 splits the mixed term at $\log y=H=L(\tfrac12\log(L/\sigma)+\kappa)$, with $L=\log N$, $\sigma=\mathfrak S(N)$ and $\kappa=\log\log L$. Under the modulus-wise GRH assumption the far interval needs a matrix of order only $O(1+(\log L)^{3/2}/\sqrt{\sigma})$ at every even modulus. On primorials, and more generally when $\log L\ll\sigma$, the entire far term tends to zero once the high-input prime-only norm $P_N(A)$ tends to zero. The remaining mixed matrix then uses primes only up to $e^{H+L}$, reducing the leading coefficient of $\log L$ in the exponent of $N$ from one to one half. Decay of $P_N(A)$ and of the remaining mixed matrix is open.

Theorem 12.3 gives a prime-only criterion for the full norm on primorial moduli under the paper's modulus-wise GRH assumption. For $N$ equal to the product of the primes up to $y$, removing every proper prime power from both arithmetic coordinates changes the form by $O((\log\log N)^{-1/2})$ in normalized operator norm whenever either normalized norm stays bounded. The supports are unrestricted. More generally the same equivalence of decay holds when $L\log(2p_0)/(p_0\mathfrak S(N)^2)\to0$, where $L=\log N$ and $p_0$ is the least prime not dividing $N$. The ordinary-prime form still includes pairs at widely separated scales, and its decay remains open even on primorials.

Theorem 12.2 gives a pointwise criterion for the full operator norm under the paper's modulus-wise GRH assumption. Put $L=\log N$ and $A=L^2(\log L)^4$. Hypothesis S in the norm formulation (1.1) holds precisely when two quantities tend to zero along all even moduli. The first is the prime-only norm $P_N(A)$ on inputs $x,y\ge A$. The second is the largest eigenvalue of an explicit positive semidefinite matrix of order $O(\sqrt L(\log L)^{3/2}\log\log L)$ for the mixed term. A grid adapted to the small prime-power weights controls the approximation error, while a covariance of signed progression sums gives the entries. Proving decay of both quantities remains open.

Theorem 11.7 returns to the operator norm in Hypothesis S. Fix $A\ge0$, $D>0$ and $\varepsilon>0$. In every interval $Y<N\le Y+H$ with $Y^{8/33+\varepsilon}\le H\le Y$, the sum over even moduli of the squared normalized norm on physical supports up to $N(\log N)^A$ is $O_{A,D,\varepsilon}(H(\log Y)^{-D})$. A single exceptional set works for all unit-energy tests, including tests chosen separately at each modulus. The proof localizes both sum-line and difference-line cell discrepancies and then bounds the norm of the resulting matrix. For the full operator the remaining form includes mixed supports and larger supports; the theorem also leaves an exceptional set of moduli.

Proposition 3.31 and Corollary 3.32 reduce the pointwise candidates of Proposition 3.30 to at most three factors under the same zero-strength assumption. For each fixed $0<\epsilon<1/20$, the positive-character prime samples with $P^-(N-p)>N^{1/4-\epsilon}$ still have mass comparable to $s=V/\eta$. After discarding $O_{a,\epsilon}(s^2)$, the partner is a prime, a product of two primes of opposite sign with the positive one above $N^{1/2-\epsilon}$, or a product of three distinct negative-character primes. The proof obtains a relative error estimate averaged over sieve moduli up to $N^{1/2-\gamma}$ for every fixed $\gamma>0$ below $1/2$. The prime contribution inside this mass remains undetermined.

Proposition 3.30 gives a pointwise estimate in every exceptional class under the additional zero-strength condition $s=V/\eta\le(\log N)^{-a}$, for fixed $a>0$ and $V=\log N/\log q\to\infty$. The distinct-prime mass satisfies $P_N=O_a(s)$. For some fixed $\rho(a)>0$, the mass of primes $p$ with $\chi(p)=1$, $\chi(N-p)=-1$ and $P^-(N-p)>N^\rho$ is bounded above and below by positive multiples of $s$. Only $O_a(s^2)$ of this mass has a positive-character prime factor of $N-p$ below or equal to $N^{2\rho}$. This locates candidates for each specified $N$; proving a positive contribution from prime partners at that $N$ remains open.

Propositions 3.28–3.29 estimate the fixed test inside each residue class where Proposition 3.26 has zero main term. Under the same exceptional-zero hypothesis, put $V=\log X/\log q$, with $V\to\infty$ and $V=o(\eta)$. For $X^{32/37+\varepsilon}\le H\le X/\log X$, both the ordinary mean of $P_N$ in each such class in $(X,X+H]$ and its mean weighted by $N\mathfrak S(N)$ are asymptotic to $2cV/\eta$. Its ordinary second moment is $O_\varepsilon((V/\eta)^2)$. Consequently a proportion bounded below independently of $q$ and $\eta$ satisfies $P_N\ge cV/\eta$ in each class and interval. The proof counts pairs sharing the same sum by fixing the prime of positive character in each representation, then sieving their two partners.

Proposition 3.26 applies the localized Matomäki–Merikoski Goldbach estimate to the fixed balanced weight, conditional on a primitive quadratic character of conductor $q$ having a real zero $1-1/(\eta\log q)$. For $12\le\log N/\log q\le\sqrt\eta$ as $\eta\to\infty$, the prime-pair mass is at least $2c/3-o(1)$ outside explicitly classified residues. All classes with zero main term belong to a single lattice of spacing at least $q/6$. In them a representation must pair a prime of character value $+1$ with one of value $-1$. Corollary 3.27 shows that a uniform positive arithmetic margin would exclude arbitrarily strong exceptional zeros for odd characters and for conductors divisible by two or three. Convergence of this margin to $c$ would exclude them for all primitive quadratic characters.

Proposition 3.25 gives positive mass with a large prime factor at the target cutoff itself. For every sufficiently large even $N$, the normalized mass with $P^-(N-p)>N^{1/5}$ and $P^+(N-p)>N^{0.512}$ exceeds $0.020c$. After the largest factor is removed, at most two prime factors remain. If there is no prime or rough semiprime partner, triples with a factor above $N^{0.558}$ must carry mass exceeding $0.005c$ for sufficiently large $N$. The proof counts the excluded two-, three- and four-factor regions separately. The lower estimate with a common largest-factor cutoff crosses zero between exponents $0.51293$ and $0.51296$. The mark of Proposition 3.19 starts at $3/5$, beyond this range.

Proposition 3.24 gives a pointwise lower bound for balanced prime samples at every sufficiently large even $N$. With $P^+(N-p)>N^{121/200}$, it raises the cutoff on the least prime factor of $N-p$ to $N^{1/50}$ and leaves at most 19 prime factors in the cofactor, counted with multiplicity. At the previous cutoff $N^{1/220}$ it raises the largest-factor exponent to $31/50$. The proof groups a marked prime with its rough cofactor before sieving the reflected sum. For each $3/5<\beta<5/8$ it gives positive mass at a sufficiently small fixed sifting exponent. Obtaining enough mass after sifting through $N^{1/5}$ remains open.

Proposition 3.23 localizes the target-cutoff estimates to every interval $(Y,Y+H]$ with $Y^{8/33+\varepsilon}\le H\le\sqrt Y$. The prime-pair mass, semiprime mass and remaining discrepancy have their predicted values up to vanishing errors outside $O_{\varepsilon,A}(H/(\log Y)^A)$ even integers in each interval. For $Y^{2/15+\varepsilon}\le H\le\sqrt Y$ their weighted means still have the same limits. At least 5% of the even integers then have prime-pair mass at least $c/2$, and at least 12% have mass at least $c/100$. These statements are unconditional for fixed positive $\varepsilon,A$ and sufficiently large $Y$. They allow individual exceptions.

Proposition 3.22 works at the target cutoff $N^{1/5}$. Sieving the product of the two prime factors together bounds the semiprime contribution by $4cI_1+o(1)<0.02383c+o(1)$ at every sufficiently large even integer. Failure of the window test therefore requires $R_N^+/c\le-0.98212+o(1)$. Theorem 10.11 bounds the mean square of the zero count in a window of radius $c_0/(K\log N)$ in terms of the high-support covariance norm. A mean square of central-zero multiplicities bounded below by a positive constant forces $\|T_N\|\gg\log N$. Under Hypothesis S the proportion of odd characters with vanishing central value is $O(1/\log\log N)$.

Theorem 10.8 bounds the proportion of odd characters with large conductor loss. With $L=\log N$, the proportion with $\log(N/q_\chi)>A(\log L)^2/\log\log L$ is at most $L^{-A+o(1)}$, with a matching exponent on primorial moduli. Under Dirichlet GRH, their contribution to the full normalized operator norm is $O_{A,\epsilon}(L^{2-A+\epsilon})$.

Theorem 10.9 gives a two-way criterion for the high-support covariance. It suffices to retain odd characters with full conductor exponent at every prime factor above $(\log N)^\rho$, for fixed $0<\rho<1$. Both directions use only nearby even divisors of $N$ and have bounded comparison constants.

Theorem 10.10 bounds the normalized short-gap part of the centered covariance by $O((1+u)L e^{-H/L})$ with a smooth product cutoff beyond $xy=e^H$, for gaps at most $u/L$ and $L=\log N$. This is a pointwise estimate at each even modulus under Dirichlet GRH. The same-label correction also has its own bound when the log supports are separated by less than $2\log N$ by the margin specified in the manuscript.

Theorem 3.2 gives a quantitative equivalence between norm decay on each fixed window $x,y<RN$ and vanishing Hardy–Littlewood discrepancies on its individual sum and difference lines. In particular, Hypothesis S would imply the expected asymptotic for prime pairs $(p,p+N)$ with $aN<p<bN$, for fixed $0<a<b$ and all even $N$ tending to infinity.

We use the Alpöge–Claude Dirichlet GRH theorem for the zero-location inputs in the manuscript. The independent verification records include theorem-statement checks and kernel replays. [Proof announcement](https://alpo.ge/remark.pdf) · [MIT verification](https://math.mit.edu/~drew/grh/) · [Waterloo verification](https://mrubinst.github.io/grh-lean-verification/)

Theorem 9.2 shows that, for any fixed $C\ge 1$, decay of the norm with the sharp product cutoff $xy\le CN^2$ is equivalent to uniform decay of signed rectangle counting discrepancies. The equivalence is unconditional for the physical arithmetic form. Under modulus-specific GRH, if $E_N$ is the full norm divided by the singular series, the sharp restriction has normalized norm $O_C(E_N^{1/3}+N^{-c})$. The improvement uses a linear upper-collar bound at fixed $C$.

Theorem 9.3 reduces this discrepancy to $O(\log N)$ prefix rectangle counts at fixed precision. Its signed continuous-model estimate allows all but a bounded number of the counts to use only the arithmetic measure, with a controlled error.

Theorem 13.2 proves unconditionally that proper prime powers contribute a vanishing mean-square norm through a fixed cap $xy\le CN^2$ across all even moduli. At a single modulus, the large proper powers have a uniform tail bound. The remaining proper-power contribution is governed by fixed opposite-residue prime counts.

A proof of Hypothesis S still needs uniform cancellation in the counts near $xy\asymp N^2$ and control of the full operator across the larger retained product range.
